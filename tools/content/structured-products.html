Understanding Structured Products

Structured products offer a way to pursue specific investment objectives through a defined set of terms. Their returns are typically linked to the performance of an underlying asset or market, such as a stock market index, a basket of shares or a combination of indices. Depending on their design, they may target regular income, capital growth or a particular balance between potential returns and investment risk.

Defined Terms, Informed Decisions

Unlike a conventional investment fund, a structured product follows a predetermined formula for calculating returns and repaying capital. This allows intermediaries to assess how a product could perform under different market conditions and consider its place within a broader portfolio. Some products offer conditional capital protection, but capital remains at risk, and outcomes depend on the product’s terms and the issuer’s ability to meet its obligations.
